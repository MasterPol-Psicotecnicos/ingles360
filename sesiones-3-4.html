Grammar

Present simple vs present continuous

Present simple

Things that are normal, regular or always true.

AffirmativeI / you / we / they work
he / she / it works
NegativeI don't work
she doesn't work
QuestionDo you work?
Does she work?
Short answerYes, I do. / No, she doesn't.

When do we use it?

  • Routines and habitsI check my emails every morning.
  • Facts and permanent situationsShe works in Human Resources.
  • Timetables and schedulesThe train leaves at 7:15.
  • State verbs (feelings, thoughts, possession)I need a coffee. He knows the answer.

Clue words

alwaysusuallyoftensometimesneverevery dayon Mondaystwice a week

Present continuous

Things happening now, or only for a period of time.

AffirmativeI am ('m) working
he / she / it is ('s) working
you / we / they are ('re) working
NegativeI'm not / she isn't / they aren't working
QuestionAre you working?
Is she working?
Short answerYes, I am. / No, she isn't.

When do we use it?

  • Actions happening nowShe's talking on the phone right now.
  • Temporary situationsI'm working from home this week.
  • Changing situationsPrices are going up.
  • Future arrangements (fixed plans)We're meeting the client on Friday.

some or any?

some in affirmative sentences: There are some boxes.

Speaking

Picture Description

Look at the picture. You will describe it for 45–60 seconds.

Where is…? What is… doing?

Complete the sentences about the picture.

How to describe a picture

Five steps
  1. General: In this picture I can see an office.
  2. Who and what: There are three people and a cat.
  3. Place: On the left / In the middle / On the right…
  4. Actions: She's talking… He's pointing… (present continuous)
  5. Your idea: I think… / It looks like…
Useful language

In this picture I can see…
There is / There are…
On the left, … · In the middle, … · On the right, …
In the background / In the foreground…
A woman is sitting at a desk next to the window.
I think they are working in an office.
It looks like a sunny day.

Model answer

In this picture I can see an office. There are three people and a cat. On the left, a woman is sitting at a desk next to the window. She's talking on the phone and she's smiling. On the desk there's a laptop and a cup of coffee, and under the desk there's a cat. It's sleeping.

In the middle, a man is standing in front of a whiteboard. He's pointing at a chart, so I think he's giving a presentation. On the right, another man is standing between a plant and a printer. He's drinking a coffee.

There's a clock above the door and there's a bin next to the desk. I think it's a sunny day, because I can see the sun through the window.

Grammar

Present perfect

Present perfect

A connection between the past and now.

AffirmativeI / you / we / they have ('ve) worked
he / she / it has ('s) worked
NegativeI haven't worked / she hasn't worked
QuestionHave you worked…? / Has she worked…?
Short answerYes, I have. / No, she hasn't.

When do we use it?

  • Life experience, no specific time (ever, never)Have you ever worked abroad? I've never been to London.
  • Started in the past and continues now (for, since)I've worked here for two years. / since 2024.
  • Recent actions with a result now (just, already, yet)I've just sent the email. Have you finished yet? She's already left.

Past participles you need

be → beendo → donego → gone / beenhave → hadmake → madesee → seensend → sentwrite → writtentake → takenbuy → boughtmeet → metfind → foundleave → leftspeak → spokengive → givenknow → knownbreak → brokeneat → eaten

Regular verbs: past participle = -ed (worked, finished, called).

Present perfect or past simple?

Present perfect
No finished time.
I've visited London twice.
Have you sent the invoice?
Past simple
Finished time: yesterday, last week, in 2020, two days ago, on Monday.
I visited London in 2022.
I sent it this morning.
for + a period
for two hours · for ten years
since + a starting point
since Monday · since 2019 · since I was 18
gone = he is there now
He's gone to Madrid.
been = he went and came back
He's been to Madrid three times.

Writing

E-mail reply

Read the following e-mail and write a reply as if you are Alex Moreno, the customer service manager of OfficeLine. Make sure you provide all the information that Karen Lewis requests. (Min. 150 words)

To: Alex Moreno <amoreno@officeline.com>
From: Karen Lewis <klewis@brightdesign.com>
Subject: Order 4471 – delivery problem

Dear Mr Moreno,

On 12 September we ordered ten office chairs and two desks from your website. We received the desks last week, but the chairs have not arrived yet. Our new employees are starting on Monday and they need somewhere to sit!

Could you tell me where the chairs are now and when exactly we can expect them? I would also like to know if you can offer us a discount for the delay.

I look forward to your prompt reply.

Kind regards,
Karen Lewis
Office Manager, Bright Design

Before you write: what does Karen ask?
Plan: 5 short paragraphs
  1. Thank her and apologise.
  2. Explain the situation now.
  3. Give the delivery day and time.
  4. Answer about the discount.
  5. Offer more help and close.

Model answer

Dear Ms Lewis,

Thank you for your email, and I am very sorry that your chairs have not arrived yet. I understand that this is a problem for you, especially because your new employees are starting on Monday.

I have checked your order (number 4471) and I have spoken to our warehouse team. Unfortunately, our supplier sent the chairs late, so they arrived at our warehouse only yesterday. At the moment, our team is preparing your order and we are sending it today by express delivery.

The chairs will arrive at your office on Friday morning, between 9 and 12. Our driver always calls the customer one hour before the delivery, so somebody can be there to receive them.

As an apology for the delay, we are happy to offer you a 15% discount on the chairs. We have already sent the refund to your card, and you will see it in three to five days.

If you need anything else, please do not hesitate to contact me.

Kind regards,
Alex Moreno
Customer Service Manager, OfficeLine
